The LazInk showcase

One page, written to be read — and to use, once each, most of what the renderer knows.

canvas-drawn 0BSD no JavaScript

LazInk draws formatted text on a plain TCanvas: labels, memos, list boxes, a page viewer and an editor, reading HTML or Markdown through one renderer. This document is ordinary HTML with a small stylesheet — the kind of page a help system or a release note actually is. Inline it handles bold, italics, underline, strikethrough, highlighted phrases, H2O and E=mc2, links, code spans, the Ctrl+F kind of key, and the small print.

How this page is used. The demo shows it beside a browser’s picture of the same file; tools/compare.sh opens it beside a live browser. Scroll either and the other follows.

Code, quoted and highlighted

A fenced block names its language and the small highlighter colors comments, strings, numbers and keywords — nothing more. A host with its own highlighter plugs in through OnHighlightCode.

The folded and the fine print

What is under this fold?

A <details> element, opened and shut by its summary — drawn and handled by LazInk, no script involved.

Entities and punctuation come out right: café, naïve, ¾ of a pie, 25 °C, x ≠ y, “curly quotes”, an em dash — and a non‑breaking hyphen.
